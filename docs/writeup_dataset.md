## Dataset Details

No external dataset is used. Following the paper, all data is synthetic and generated by our own simulator, which lets us produce unlimited labelled samples with exact ground truth.

**Aircraft model.** The leader is modelled as 14 known structural feature points (nose, wingtips, tail fin tip, stabiliser tips, exhaust, etc.) on a fighter-like airframe about 15 m long with a 10 m wingspan. Each point has an outward surface normal.

**Sample generation.** For each sample we draw a random relative pose uniformly within the paper's ranges: forward offset −100 to 50 m, lateral −50 to 50 m, vertical −20 to 20 m, and relative roll −45° to 45°. Relative pitch and yaw are drawn within ±5° as nuisance variation. Poses closer than 15 m are rejected as collisions. The 14 points are then projected through a pinhole camera (1280 × 960 px, 40° field of view) that always points at the leader. A point is marked occluded when its surface faces away from the camera, and visible points get 1 px Gaussian noise to mimic an imperfect detector.

**Features and labels.** Each sample is a 42-value vector: x pixel, y pixel and an occlusion flag for each of the 14 points, with occluded points set to zero. The label is one of 4800 pose bins (10 × 10 × 8 × 6 over x, y, z and roll), exactly as in the paper's Table I.

**Size and split.** We generated 400,000 samples, split 80/20 into 320,000 training and 80,000 validation samples with a fixed random seed. 4,773 of the 4,800 bins appear; the remaining 27 lie within 15 m of the leader and are excluded by the collision filter. On average 4.5 of the 14 points are occluded per sample. Features are standardised using training-set statistics before being fed to the network.
